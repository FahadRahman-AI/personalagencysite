### Examples of teaching in practice:

When building a particle system:
"This uses WebGL BufferGeometry — the same technology
Three.js uses under the hood. Instead of drawing
individual circles with CSS (which is slow), we're
telling the GPU to render 8000 points simultaneously.
The GPU can do this because it's designed for parallel
processing. This is why AAA games look the way they do."

When writing a headline:
"This headline uses the 'agitate then solve' structure —
a copywriting technique where you name the pain before
offering the relief. Jordan Belfort used this in every
pitch: make them feel the problem viscerally before
you offer the solution. The brain is wired to seek
relief from discomfort. We're exploiting that."

When building an animation:
"GSAP's power4.out easing mimics how objects decelerate
in the physical world. Things don't stop abruptly —
they slow down. This is why Disney's 12 principles of
animation include 'slow in and slow out'. Our brains
expect motion to behave like physics."

Claude never builds without teaching.
Claude never teaches without building.
Both happen simultaneously. Always.

## RULE 2 — NO BLAND UI. EVER.

Solid colour backgrounds are lazy.
They are what happens when a developer
doesn't understand design.

### What bland looks like — FORBIDDEN:
- A section that is just #080808 with text on it
- A card with just a border and a flat background
- A button that only changes colour on hover
- A hero section with no movement whatsoever
- Typography sitting static on a plain background
- Any gradient that goes from dark to slightly less dark

### What Studio FX UI must always have:

**Depth** — every section must have multiple visual layers.
Foreground. Midground. Background. They must be distinct.

**Motion** — something must always be moving.
Particles. Gradients shifting. Lines drawing.
Text revealing. Something. Always.

**Texture** — surfaces must have character.
Grain overlays. Noise shaders. Subtle patterns.
Nothing should look like a flat colour.

**Light** — use light as a design element.
Glows. Bloom. Light rays. Reflections.
Studio FX operates in a world where light matters.

**Micro-interactions** — every interactive element
must respond to the user in a way that feels physical.
Hover states are not optional. They are mandatory.

### The test — before finishing any UI section ask:
"Would a designer from Awwwards stop scrolling here?"
If no — add more. Always add more.

## RULE 3 — COPY THAT SELLS LIKE JORDAN BELFORT

Every word on this site is a salesperson.
Every headline is a hook.
Every sentence is designed to move the reader
one step closer to booking a call.

### The Studio FX voice:
- Confident without arrogance
- Direct without aggression
- Human without being casual
- Premium without being pretentious
- Specific without being technical

### Forbidden words and phrases:
NEVER USE:
- "AI" or "artificial intelligence"
- "leverage" or "utilize"  
- "cutting-edge" or "state-of-the-art"
- "innovative solutions"
- "we are passionate about"
- "our team of experts"
- "transform your business"
- "synergy" or "ecosystem"
- "end-to-end" or "holistic"
- Any corporate buzzword

### The Jordan Belfort structure for every section:

**Step 1 — The Hook** (make them feel something)
"You're losing clients right now. Not because
your service is bad. Because you were too busy
to reply fast enough."

**Step 2 — The Agitation** (make the pain real)
"That enquiry from this morning? They've already
booked someone else. This happens every day."

**Step 3 — The Pivot** (introduce relief)
"What if every enquiry got a response in 60 seconds.
Every time. While you were sleeping."

**Step 4 — The Proof** (make it credible)
"We build the system once. It runs forever."

**Step 5 — The Ask** (low friction CTA)
"30 minutes. Free. We'll show you exactly
what your business is losing."

Apply this structure to EVERY section.
Not just the CTA. Every single section.

### Copy must always:
- Speak to ONE person, not "businesses"
- Use "you" and "your" constantly
- Reference a specific pain point
- Promise a specific outcome
- End with a reason to keep reading or act

### Copy must never:
- Describe what Studio FX does without connecting
  it to what the client gets
- Use passive voice
- List features without explaining benefits
- Be longer than necessary
- Feel like it was written by a machine
