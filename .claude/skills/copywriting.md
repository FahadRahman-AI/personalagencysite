# Studio FX — Copywriting & Content Skill

## Identity
Studio FX is a Visual Elevation Studio based in Birmingham, UK, serving ambitious businesses worldwide. We build websites, AI-powered systems, and cinematic content that make businesses look and operate like they belong in a completely different league to their competition.

**Tagline:** "We make your business look like it raised a Series A."
**Primary CTA:** Book a free 30-minute Brand Elevation Call
**Contact:** hello@studiofx.co

## Tone of Voice
- Confident without arrogance
- Direct without being aggressive
- Premium without being pretentious
- Human without being casual
- Never use: "synergy", "optimize", "empower", "transform", "end-to-end", "leverage", "innovative solutions"
- Never mention "AI" directly to cold audiences — say "smart systems", "automated workflows", "background processes"
- Write like a person, not a brand
- Short sentences. Strong verbs. No filler.

## Target Client Profile
A business owner who:
- Knows their product or service is genuinely good
- Feels their online presence undersells them
- Watches competitors who are less capable win clients because they look more credible
- Has been quoted either too cheap (feels unprofessional) or too expensive (agency markup) for web work
- Makes decisions based on trust and taste, not features and pricing tables
- Is based anywhere in the world but especially UK, UAE, and US markets

## The Core Pain Point
"My business looks nothing like how good it actually is."

Every piece of copy must connect back to this pain. The visitor should read the headline and think — yes, that is exactly how I feel.

## Psychological Hooks To Use
1. **The gap** — show the distance between how they look now and how they could look
2. **The competitor mirror** — make them think about rivals who look better but are less capable
3. **The inevitability** — make booking a call feel like the obvious next step, not a risk
4. **The specificity** — reference their actual situation, never speak generically
5. **The social proof of restraint** — "we only take on projects we believe in" signals selectivity

## Services (Never list as bullet features — always frame as outcomes)
- **Websites:** "Your customers decide in 3 seconds. We make those 3 seconds count."
- **AI Systems:** "Admin that used to take hours runs itself. You focus on the work that actually makes money."
- **Cinematic Content:** "Footage that makes people stop scrolling and start paying attention."

## Pricing Philosophy (for copy purposes)
Never mention prices in copy. Position as investment not cost. The free call removes all financial friction from the first step.

## Section-by-Section Copy Rules

### Hero Section
- Lead with the visitor's internal monologue, not a service description
- One bold statement that makes them feel seen
- Subheading that agitates the gap
- CTA that removes friction: "Book a free elevation call"
- No more than 30 words total above the fold

### Problem Section
- Name the specific frustration without being dramatic
- Use "you" language — speak directly to one person
- Never use statistics unless they are verifiable and specific
- Three problems maximum — more feels like a list, not a mirror

### Social Proof
- Lead with the outcome, not the client name
- One line testimonial format: what changed, not what we did
- If no testimonials yet: use honest scarcity — "currently building case studies with founding clients"

### CTA Sections
- Never "Contact Us" — always action-oriented and specific
- "Book Your Free Elevation Call" — primary
- "See What's Possible" — secondary for undecided visitors
- Below every CTA: remove the last objection — "30 minutes. No commitment. Just vision."

## Writing Process For Every Section
Before writing any copy, answer:
1. What is the visitor feeling when they arrive at this section?
2. What do we want them to feel when they leave it?
3. What is the one thing they must understand?
4. What is the one action they must take?

Only then write the copy.

## Forbidden Patterns
- Never open with "We are Studio FX" or any brand-first statement
- Never list services as bullet points
- Never use passive voice
- Never end a section without a clear next step
- Never write copy that could apply to any other agency — every line must be specific to Studio FX's position
- Never mention technology stacks to cold audiences (no "Next.js", "n8n", "Claude API")

## Quality Check — Before Finalising Any Copy Ask:
- Would a cold visitor who has never heard of Studio FX understand this immediately?
- Does it make them feel something or just convey information?
- Could this copy appear on any other agency's site? If yes, rewrite it.
- Is the CTA so clear and low-friction that saying no feels strange?
